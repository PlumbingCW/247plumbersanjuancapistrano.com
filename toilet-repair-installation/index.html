The tank that keeps running

A running toilet wastes more water than most people expect, because it fills and drains quietly all day. Three parts cause it. The flapper does not seal the drain hole, the fill valve does not shut off at the right level, or the float is set too high so the overflow tube carries the extra.

Drop dye in the tank and wait. Color in the bowl without a flush means the flapper is leaking. Hard water, averaging 12 grains per gallon in the district's 2025 report, leaves scale on the flapper seat and the fill valve, so a cleaned seat or a new rubber part often brings it back.

A rocking toilet and a wet base

Under the bowl, a wax ring seals the toilet to the drain flange in the floor. When that seal fails, water seeps out at the base on every flush, and you may smell sewer gas. A loose toilet breaks the ring over time, which is why a rocking bowl is a warning.

The fix is to pull the toilet, scrape off the old wax, inspect the flange and set the toilet down on a fresh ring. If the flange is cracked or sits too low, it gets repaired first, because a new ring cannot seal on a broken ledge. Soft flooring around the base means water has been getting in for a while.

Supply valve and supply line failures

The small valve behind the toilet and the braided line to the tank are the parts that most often fail without warning. A stop valve that has not been turned for years may weep from its stem. A braided line can corrode at the nut and burst, which is the source of many sudden flood calls. Replacing both when you replace anything else is cheap protection, and the plumber can show you the old parts.

Replacing a toilet from start to finish

  1. STEP 01

    Choose the fit.

    The distance from the wall to the flange center, called the rough-in, decides which bowl fits. Most are the same, but old homes can differ.

  2. STEP 02

    Remove the old toilet.

    The water is shut off, the tank drained, and the bowl lifted free so the flange can be checked.

  3. STEP 03

    Set the new one.

    A new wax ring or seal goes on, the bowl is leveled and bolted down without cracking the porcelain.

  4. STEP 04

    Connect and test.

    A fresh supply line is attached, the tank filled, and the unit flushed several times while the base is watched for seepage.
