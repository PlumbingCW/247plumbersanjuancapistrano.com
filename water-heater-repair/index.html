Fitting, valve or tank wall: finding where the leak starts

A plumber follows the water uphill. Rust trails, wet insulation and mineral crust show whether a drip began at a connection or is weeping through the steel itself. That one finding decides whether you need a twenty-minute fix or a new heater.

Why the T&P valve matters

Think of it as the tank's emergency exit. When heat or pressure climbs too far, the valve opens and dumps water so the steel cannot burst. A valve that drips all the time is worn or answering a real pressure problem, so it gets tested, never capped.

What it sounds like at your house

  • The shower starts hot, then fades to lukewarm after a couple of minutes.
  • There is popping or rumbling from the tank whenever it heats.
  • The floor around the heater is damp and I cannot tell from where.
  • Only the hot taps run rusty or brown, and the cold taps are clear.
  • The pilot light keeps going out, or the flame looks yellow and lazy.
  • The breaker for the water heater trips every time it kicks on.
  • The hot water smells like rotten eggs.

No hot water at all: how each type of heater gets checked

On a gas heater the usual suspects are a dirty pilot assembly, a worn thermocouple or a gas control valve that has quit. A technician tests each one with the flame running instead of swapping parts and hoping. Gas smell is a different matter: leave the house and call the gas company first.

An electric tank has two heating elements and two thermostats, and either element can fail alone. That is why some homes get lukewarm water instead of none. A tripped high-limit reset button is also worth a look, since it often trips because something else is failing.

Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor, a fan and an air filter. When those struggle, the unit falls back on its backup elements, so your bill climbs before the hot water disappears. We read the fault code, then test the parts it points to.

Scale on the elements is a San Juan Capistrano habit

Santa Margarita Water District reported 2025 hardness averaging 210 mg/L, about 12 grains per gallon, with readings up to 290 mg/L in parts of the system. Calcium that heavy bakes onto an electric element like a shell, so the metal overheats and burns out. If you have already replaced one element in a few years, that is the reason.

So a repair visit should include a look at the tank bottom. Flushing loose sediment while the element is open adds little time and protects the new part. A gas heater with the same buildup tends to rumble, which is steam bursting up through the scale.

Straps, clearance and the safety check we do while we are there

California requires a tank water heater to be braced or strapped so an earthquake cannot tip it and snap the gas or water lines. A repair visit is a good moment to confirm the straps are snug. Loose straps are cheap to correct and expensive to ignore.
