A pinhole looks harmless, but it is usually the first visible sign that the pipe wall is thin along its length. The question after any repair is whether this was a one-time failure or the start of a pattern.

Copper pipe repair

Copper is a common supply pipe, and it fails in a few predictable ways. Corrosion pits the inside wall, a joint lets go because of an old solder flaw, or a kink from past work eventually splits. Hard water, averaging 12 grains per gallon in SMWD's 2025 report, leaves scale in lines and heaters, and scale hastens wear where flow is rough.

Repair means cutting out the damaged length, cleaning the ends, and joining new copper with soldered or pressed fittings. Press fittings avoid open flame, which matters near dry framing. Where the rest of the line is the same age, the plumber will tell you what else looks tired, and repiping comes up when it does.

Why a pipe bursts where hard freezes are rare

With a mean daily low around 56 F, freeze damage is uncommon in San Juan Capistrano, though hard freezes are not impossible. Most local bursts have other roots. Pressure spikes slam fittings, corroded sections give way, and shifting soil stresses a buried pipe. A knock or bang in the walls when a valve closes is worth a look before it becomes a split.

Broken drain pipes and wet ground

Drain pipes break differently. Water does not spray, it seeps, and the first signs are slow fixtures, sewage smell, a patch of soggy ground or a bump in the lawn. Tree roots, ground movement and aged pipe walls are the usual causes.

A camera inspection shows the crack before digging. If the break is a single stretch, that section is replaced. Stopping water use in the house keeps the problem from spreading into the yard while you wait.

Signs a pipe is about to fail

  • There is a damp spot on a wall that returns after I wipe it.
  • The paint is bubbling above a baseboard.
  • I see green or white crust on a copper fitting.
  • The pipes bang when the washing machine shuts off.
  • My water looks rusty for the first minute.
  • A small drip under the sink never stops.

How the repair is carried out

  1. STEP 01

    Stop and drain.

    The main is closed and the line is drained so the plumber can work in a dry pipe.

  2. STEP 02

    Open and expose.

    A small section of drywall or soil is removed to reach the failure and sound pipe on both sides.

  3. STEP 03

    Replace the section.

    The damaged length comes out, new pipe and fittings go in, and nearby joints are inspected.

  4. STEP 04

    Pressurize and check.

    Water is turned back on slowly, the repair watched for seepage, and the area left ready for drying and patching.
