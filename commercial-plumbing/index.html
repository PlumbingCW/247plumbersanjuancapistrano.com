A commercial plumbing emergency after the doors close

Commercial plumbing in San Juan Capistrano, CA covers the pipes, drains, fixtures and water-side equipment in stores, offices, restaurants and multi-tenant buildings. A failure at closing time is an emergency because water spreads into shared walls and neighboring suites quickly. The 24/7 line is (949) 325-3122, and the first minutes before a plumber is on site matter.

Beyond local testing rules, the California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) is the baseline for commercial fixtures, drains and backflow devices.

Signs the building needs a plumber tonight

  • Sewage or gray water is coming up in a floor drain or restroom.
  • Several fixtures gurgle or drain slowly at the same time.
  • The water meter keeps moving after every tap is shut.
  • A ceiling tile is stained, sagging or dripping.
  • A kitchen smells of grease from the floor drain.
  • The water bill jumped and nobody can explain why.
  • A water heater or hot water return is leaking in a back room.

One slow fixture is a nuisance, but several at once point to a shared line. If the main sewer is struggling, every fixture downstream will back up in order from the lowest point.

Commercial drains and back-ups

Commercial drain lines run longer and carry heavier loads than a house line. Paper towels, wipes and kitchen waste build up in the same few bends. A cable machine with a heavier head, or a camera to see what is blocking the pipe, is usually the right first step.

When the pattern repeats, the line may need jetting or a section repaired rather than another quick clearing. How clearing works is laid out on the page on how lines get cleared, and a building with a history of back-ups may need scheduled maintenance as well.

Commercial leak detection: what to expect

Finding a hidden leak in a commercial building takes equipment, access and time, and it does not always end with a repair. Acoustic listening gear, thermal imaging and pressure tests narrow the search. Some leaks sit above ceilings, inside shared walls or under a floor slab that the tenant does not control.

That means detection needs coordination. The building owner may need to approve opening a wall, a neighboring tenant may have to give access, and some areas can only be reached after hours. A visit can confirm that a leak exists and roughly where, or it can rule out the suspect line, and both results are useful.

We will tell you plainly if a leak is too hidden to pinpoint without opening finishes, or if a high bill turns out to be a stuck fixture and no leak at all. The methods are described further on our page about finding hidden leaks.

Grease trap cleaning for kitchens

A grease trap catches fats and food solids before they leave a kitchen, and once it fills the baffles stop working and grease passes into the line. Symptoms are a sour smell, slow floor drains and sinks that back up when the dishwasher drains. A full trap must be pumped and the lid and baffles inspected, not just topped up.

How often a trap needs service depends on kitchen volume and trap size. Ask the city or the district that serves your address what your kitchen's cleaning and record-keeping requirements are.

Backflow testing: who tests and when

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from flowing backward into the drinking supply. Many commercial properties have one on the main service, on irrigation, or on a fire line. Testing requirements and who is allowed to perform the test vary, so ask the water district or the city what applies to your property.

If a test fails or a device leaks, a plumber can repair or replace it, and a retest follows. Keep the paperwork, since landlords and tenants both get asked for it during inspections.

Storm drains on the property

Parking-lot drains and roof leaders carry rainwater away, and leaves, trash and silt clog them. San Juan Capistrano gets about 13.6 inches of rain a year, so a blocked drain may sit unnoticed until the first heavy storm. Walk the lot before the wet season and tell us about any pooling.
