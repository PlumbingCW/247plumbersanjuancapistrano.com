Repiping in San Juan Capistrano, CA

Repiping replaces the supply pipes that carry water through a home. This page explains when it makes sense, what the material choices mean, and what the job involves.

What repiping means for a home

Repiping in San Juan Capistrano, CA means replacing the water supply lines inside a house, from the main shutoff to each fixture, with new pipe. It does not usually include the drain and sewer side. It is planned work, so you can compare options and pick a date, and a short note applies: if a pipe is actively bursting, call the 24/7 line at (949) 325-3122, since burst pipe repair is handled as an emergency.

The decision is rarely made over one leak. It comes from a pattern: repeated repairs, discolored water, weak flow, or pipe that has aged out of its useful life. A repipe is rebuilt to the California Plumbing Code (UPC-based), which is a good reason to hire a licensed contractor rather than a handyman.

Signs the pipes are wearing out

  • Water runs brown or rusty when it sits overnight.
  • Showers have lost pressure but the street supply is fine.
  • We keep fixing pinhole leaks in different places.
  • Green stains or crusty deposits show on exposed pipe joints.
  • Hot water takes forever to arrive and then sputters.
  • A plumber told us the pipe is galvanized steel.
  • The walls have been opened twice already for leaks.

One of these signs alone may have a cheaper answer. Three or four together usually mean the pipes are failing as a system, and patching each leak is only buying time.

Galvanized pipe and why it gets replaced

Galvanized steel pipe is coated in zinc that wears off. Once it does, the steel rusts from the inside, and the bore narrows until flow drops. A pipe can look fine outside and be nearly closed inside.

We do not guess what is behind your walls. A plumber can look at accessible sections, check flow at fixtures, and tell you whether the visible pipe is galvanized, copper or plastic. Pipe material depends on the building, not the street or the ZIP code.

Hard water and what it does to pipes and fixtures

SMWD's 2026 report for San Juan Capistrano gives an average about 12 grains per gallon of hardness for 2025, with a range from about 5.2 to 17 depending on where you live in the system. That is hard water. It leaves scale in water heaters, on faucet aerators and inside valves, whatever pipe you choose.

For a repipe, that means new valves and fixture connections will scale over time, so quality shutoff valves and a yearly flush of the water heater are sensible. If scale is a recurring complaint, a softener may be worth a look, and the page on water softeners and filters explains the options. To see the number for your own street, read the district's water quality report or ask SMWD Customer Care.

How a whole-house repipe goes

  1. STEP 01

    Walk-through.

    The plumber maps fixtures, the water heater, the main shutoff and where walls or crawlspaces allow access.

  2. STEP 02

    Plan and price.

    You hear the pipe material, the routing, and a clear price before work starts.

  3. STEP 03

    Water off, new lines in.

    Old supply is cut out and new pipe is run through the framing. The water is off for part of each work day.

  4. STEP 04

    Pressure test.

    The new system is filled and pressure tested before any wall is closed.

  5. STEP 05

    Patch and clean-up.

    Access holes are patched to drywall stage. Paint and finishes are usually a separate trade.

A whole house repipe is measured in days, not hours, and the length depends on the size of the home and how easy the walls are to reach. Permit and inspection needs differ from job to job, so check with the city's building department before the date is set.
