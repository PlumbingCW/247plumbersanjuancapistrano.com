Repair or replace: the call a plumber makes at the tank

Not every failing heater needs to go. The question is whether the tank shell is sound and whether the money you would spend on parts is better spent on a new unit. A short repair visit is the right answer when only a valve, element or thermostat has quit.

How a same-day swap goes

When a matching unit and an open crew line up, the swap can happen the same day, and many are done in one visit. If your heater is an unusual size or fuel type, the plumber may need to bring a different model back. Swapping a tank is still plumbing work under the California Plumbing Code, a version of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so the relief valve and strapping are part of the job.

  1. STEP 01

    Shut down and drain.

    Water, power and gas are isolated, then the old tank is emptied through a hose to a safe drain.

  2. STEP 02

    Disconnect and haul out.

    Supply lines, flue or vent, and gas or electric connections come off, and the dead tank leaves with the crew.

  3. STEP 03

    Set and reconnect.

    The new heater goes on the stand or pan, with new supply connectors, a fresh relief valve line and the venting or wiring matched to the fuel.

  4. STEP 04

    Strap and test.

    Straps are fitted to meet California bracing rules, the tank is filled, bled of air, fired up and checked for leaks and temperature.

Why tanks in this part of Orange County give out sooner

Tank heaters last roughly a decade, and local water shortens that. The 2025 Santa Margarita Water District average was 210 mg/L, around 12 grains per gallon, and some readings reached 290 mg/L. Calcium that hard settles on the tank floor, covers the element or burner area and eats into the sacrificial anode that protects the steel.

If your old tank went quicker than its warranty paper suggested, scale is a fair suspect. For the new one, ask the plumber about flushing every year or so and checking the anode, or pair it with a softener if hardness bothers you elsewhere in the house.

Gas to electric, electric to gas, or like for like

Like for like is quickest, because the gas line, vent or circuit is already there. Switching fuel is possible, but it means a new gas line and flue, or a heavier electrical circuit, and sometimes a different spot for the heater. Decide before the old tank comes out, since changing your mind halfway adds a second trip.

Heat pump water heaters are the third option. They pull warmth from surrounding air to heat the tank, so they use far less electricity than a plain element, but they need floor space, a bit of airflow and a drain for condensate. They suit a garage or large utility room better than a closet.

Signs the tank is at the end

  • Rust-colored water only on the hot side, even after running it a while.
  • A small puddle comes back every few days, no matter how often I wipe it up.
  • The tank base looks swollen or the metal skirt is flaking.
  • I keep paying to fix this heater and something else breaks.
  • It is the original heater and the house has had it for more than ten years.
  • Loud banging each time the water starts heating.
