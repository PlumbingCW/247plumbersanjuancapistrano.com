Plumbing on a street that is older than plumbing codes

Los Rios Street runs beside the Mission and the train station, with addresses from 31600 to 31921. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on April 4, 1983. The district covers about 6.2 acres and lists 31 buildings.

People who live here know the street is special. Plumbing in a house like that is a different job from plumbing in a tract home, because the work has to respect the building as well as fix the problem. Call (949) 325-3122, give us the street number, and pass on anything a past plumber told you.

We cover the usual calls here: slow drains, leaks, tired water heaters and backed-up sewer lines. What changes is the order we do things in. We look first, explain what we see, and only then talk about the repair.

What is known about Los Rios Street, and what is not

  • Known: the street holds a National Register historic district listed in 1983, with 31 buildings counted in the listing.
  • Known: the district once had about 40 adobe structures, and three adobes remain today.
  • Known: many of the other houses are wooden board-and-batten buildings that replaced lost adobes in the 19th century.
  • Not known to us: what pipe material is in any one house, or which houses have been re-plumbed.
  • Not known to us: who owns the sewer lateral at your address, so confirm that with the district that serves you.

We list the unknowns on purpose. A page that guessed at your pipes would be selling you a story. A camera and a pressure check tell you the truth in an afternoon.

Narrow street, mature trees, small houses: how a visit works

The street is narrow, has no sidewalks, and has large mature trees. Many houses sit back from the road and are small. That affects the job in plain ways, so mention them when you call.

  • Tell us where a work vehicle can stop without blocking a neighbor or a driveway.
  • Tell us how to reach the main shutoff and the water heater, because small houses tuck them into tight spots.
  • Clear a path to the problem fixture if you can do it safely.
  • Mention any pets or visitors who might be in the way.

Large trees are lovely, and their roots are also a general cause of sewer line trouble anywhere. If a drain slows down over and over, a camera inspection of the sewer line shows whether roots, a sag or a crack is behind it.

Water heaters in a small historic house

Small houses often leave little room for a water heater. Before any replacement we check the space, the venting and the bracing. California requires water heaters to be braced or strapped against earthquake movement, and that rule applies to an old house as much as a new one.

Hard water matters here too, because the city's water district reports hard water on average. Scale builds on heating parts and tank bottoms, so ask us about softening or filtration when we quote a new unit.

Fix, repair or replace: a sensible order for older homes

  1. STEP 01

    Look first.

    A camera inspection and a pressure check show what is actually failing.

  2. STEP 02

    Repair what can be repaired.

    A failed section is not the same as a failed house. Targeted repair keeps more of the original building.

  3. STEP 03

    Replace with a plan.

    If repairs keep stacking up, we price a section or whole-house replacement so you can compare it with the repair bills.

Before you plan a larger job, check with city staff on what approvals apply to your lot, especially for anything visible from the street. For a business question nearby, see our page for the Historic Town Center.

In a historic house, any pipe work still falls under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code. You can see everything we handle across San Juan Capistrano on our home page.
