What a camera inspection tells you that a clean-out cannot

A sewer inspection in San Juan Capistrano, CA is a camera run through the line from the house toward the street, recorded so you can watch the pipe walls, joints and any blockage. A clean-out cap only shows the first few feet, while the camera follows the line around bends to the lower end. Planned scopes are scheduled visits, and urgent failures go to the 24/7 line at (949) 325-3122.

The plumber watches the live feed, marks anything unusual and notes the distance from the access point. You leave with the recording and a plain explanation of it. If something needs attention, sewer line repair is the follow-up.

Scoping a house before you close

Buyers rarely see the sewer line, and a standard home inspection does not run a camera through it. A scope during the inspection period shows roots, cracks or sagging sections before they become your problem. It also gives you real information to use in a negotiation.

If you are an investor or a property manager taking over a building, the same logic applies. Ask the seller for any past sewer records, then use the recording to compare. The result can be a repair credit, a request for the seller to fix it, or a decision to walk away.

Locating the line when no one knows where it runs

Additions, old remodels and buried clean-outs can leave a property with no clear route for the sewer. A camera with a transmitter sends a signal that a locator picks up above ground, so the plumber can mark the path and depth in paint or flags.

That marking helps with planning a patio, a pool or a fence, and it tells a repair crew where to dig if a repair is needed. In buildings that have been altered over many years, such as those around Los Rios Street, plans may not match what is in the ground. A locate settles the question.

Some findings need action and some only need watching. A good plumber tells you which is which instead of selling every repair. Roots and grease usually lead to cleaning the line or to jetting, while broken pipe leads to repair.

Reasons to scope the line that have nothing to do with a sale

  • Our drains clear and then clog again within weeks.
  • A cable stopped at the same point twice.
  • The lawn above the line is wet or sunken.
  • I am about to put in a pool, addition or heavy landscaping.
  • Sewer smell lingers outside near the cleanout.
  • The house is old and I do not know what is below it.

Any of these is worth a look before spending on cleaning or digging. A camera replaces guessing with evidence, which often keeps a small problem small.
