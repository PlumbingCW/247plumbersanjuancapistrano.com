Most disposal calls turn out to be one of three things: a jam, a tripped overload, or a leaking seal. Each has a different fix, which is why the first description you give matters. If the schedule has room, we can often set up a visit the same day, and active leaks are handled first on the after-hours line.

What the sink is telling you

  • It hums when I flip the switch but nothing spins.
  • The switch does nothing at all, no sound.
  • Water sits in the sink and drains very slowly after I grind.
  • There is a puddle in the cabinet that was not there yesterday.
  • It smells like old food even after I run cold water.
  • The dishwasher leaves dirty water in the bottom after a cycle.
  • I hear metal rattling and I think something fell in.

A hum with no spin is a jam almost every time. Silence usually means a tripped reset button or a dead motor. Wet cabinets and slow drains point elsewhere, and the next sections explain how a plumber tells them apart.

Why a disposal hums and will not spin

Inside the housing, a spinning plate with two swinging lugs throws food against a fixed grind ring. A bone, fruit pit or bottle cap can wedge between the plate and the ring. The motor then draws current against a locked shaft, hums, and trips its thermal overload.

A plumber frees the plate from underneath with a hex wrench, or from above with a wooden spoon handle while the power is off. After that, the reset button on the bottom of the unit gets pressed. If the unit jams again right away, the motor bearings or the plate are worn and replacement is the sensible route.

Finding where the water is coming from

Disposal leaks come from three places. The sink flange at the top leaks when the mounting ring loosens or the putty seal dries out. The body seams leak when the housing corrodes from inside. The side connections leak where the drain pipe or the dishwasher hose clamps to the unit.

Dry the whole unit, wrap a paper towel around each joint, and run water for a minute. The towel that darkens first names the culprit. Flange and connection leaks are often repairable, but a seam leak means the housing is finished and cannot be patched.

Dishwasher hookup and drain trouble

The dishwasher drain hose ties into a small inlet on the side of the disposal. New units ship with a knockout plug blocking that inlet, and when someone forgets to punch it out, the dishwasher cannot empty. The hose should also loop high under the counter so sink water does not siphon back into the machine.

San Juan Capistrano tap water averages about 12 grains per gallon of hardness, and mineral scale builds up in small drain fittings over time. That scale can narrow the dishwasher inlet and the air gap, so a hookup that is slow to drain is worth checking even when the disposal itself runs fine. If the pipe beyond the disposal is the real clog, our drain cleaning crew can clear the line.
