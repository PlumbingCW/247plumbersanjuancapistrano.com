The drip that will not stop: cartridges, washers and seats

A faucet is a valve with a handle. Inside is a cartridge or a rubber washer pressed against a metal seat, and when the seal wears or the seat pits, water slips past. That is the drip. It is rarely the handle that has failed.

Local water is hard, averaging 12 grains per gallon in SMWD's 2025 report. Scale grows on the seat and in the aerator, which both roughens the sealing surface and cuts the stream. If your faucet sprays sideways or runs thin, unscrew the aerator and soak it first, then call if the drip is the real problem.

Supply stops under the sink that will not turn or will not seal

Those small valves under sinks and behind toilets are called angle stops. Many go years without being turned, and then they seize, weep from the stem, or snap when someone forces them. A stop that leaks when you touch it should be replaced, not tightened.

Replacing one means shutting the house water, cutting off the old valve, and fitting a new one with a fresh supply line. When a stop will not close at all, the main must be shut for the whole job, which is why the quick-reference photo of your main valve is worth taking now.

Shower valve and showerhead problems

  • The shower will not shut off all the way.
  • I can only get hot or cold, never a mix.
  • The handle is stiff or spins without doing anything.
  • The showerhead drips for minutes after I turn it off.
  • Water sprays sideways from the head.
  • There is a wet spot on the wall behind the shower.

The temperature mixing happens in a cartridge behind the handle. A worn cartridge gives lukewarm drift or a continual drip at the head, and a scaled one sticks. A showerhead that sprays crooked is often just a clogged nozzle, which a vinegar soak can clear.

Bathtub spouts and the diverter that gives up

The diverter is the lift knob or pull rod that sends water up to the showerhead. When it wears, half the flow keeps coming out of the tub spout while you shower. A tub faucet that leaks while off usually shares the same cause as a kitchen drip, a worn cartridge or washer.

Slow shower and tub drains

A shower that pools around your ankles is usually hair and soap forming a plug in the trap just below the drain. A strainer pulled out and cleaned often solves it. If water backs up in more than one fixture, or gurgles, the trouble is deeper and belongs with drain cleaning.
