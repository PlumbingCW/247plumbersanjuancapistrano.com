What a proper water heater install includes

Water heater installation in San Juan Capistrano, CA means choosing a unit that fits your household, setting it where it can be vented and serviced, and connecting water, fuel and safety devices to code. A good install is mostly decisions made before the old tank moves. Those decisions are covered below so you know what to ask.

If your current tank has already failed, emergency replacement is the faster route. This page is for planned work: a remodel, a new build, a move from gas to electric, or an upgrade you chose to do on your own timeline. Urgent failures still go to the 24/7 line at (949) 325-3122. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), is the rulebook the plumber works from for relief valves, venting and gas connections.

Sizing: the household decides, not the closet

Tank size and recovery rate are two separate numbers. A 40-gallon tank with a fast burner can outperform a 50-gallon tank with a slow one in a busy morning. What matters is how many people shower close together, whether a soaking tub sits upstairs and how far the heater is from the farthest fixture.

What pushes the size up or down.
SituationSizing effect
Several showers in the same hourLarger tank or higher recovery.
Large soaking tub or multiple body spraysLarger tank, or a second heater near the bathroom.
Long pipe run from heater to bathConsider recirculation or a closer location.
Two people, one bathroomSmaller tank is fine and uses less standby energy.
Scale buildup in the tank over the yearsLeave some margin, because sediment takes up capacity.

That last row is local. Santa Margarita Water District reported a 2025 average hardness of 210 mg/L, about 12 grains per gallon, and the range ran to 290 mg/L. Scale piles up at the bottom of the tank and quietly shrinks the usable hot water, so we lean toward slightly more capacity instead of the bare minimum.

Venting, gas lines and combustion air

A gas heater needs a vent that pulls exhaust outdoors with the right rise and slope, plus enough combustion air in the room. A closet with a louvered door is not always enough once the burner is running. A cramped or sealed space may need a direct-vent or power-vent model.

The gas line matters as much as the vent. If the supply pipe is too small for the new burner, the heater starves at high demand. Electric models skip the vent but need a dedicated circuit sized to the elements, which is a job for the electrician if the panel is full.

The expansion tank most homes need

Water expands when it heats. In a house with a check valve or pressure regulator at the meter, that water has nowhere to go, so pressure spikes every time the heater fires. A small expansion tank on the cold line absorbs the swell and spares the T&P valve, fixtures and supply hoses.

Without one, you may see the relief valve dripping or washing machine hoses burst without any visible reason. We test your static pressure and check whether the system is closed, then set the expansion tank's air charge to match.

Straps, permits and the building department

Every tank in this state has to be braced or strapped so a quake cannot walk it off its base. Typically that is a strap near the top and another near the bottom, screwed into studs, or a kit made for that model. Skipping it risks a tipped heater ripping gas and water lines out of the wall.

Permit rules vary, so ask the San Juan Capistrano building department. We will describe the work involved so you can ask the right question.

Install day, step by step

  1. STEP 01

    Walk the site.

    The plumber looks at the heater location, vent route, gas or electrical capacity and shutoff valves.

  2. STEP 02

    Prepare the space.

    Water is shut off, the old unit is drained and removed, and the pad, pan or platform is set.

  3. STEP 03

    Set and connect.

    The new heater goes in, with supply lines, relief valve, discharge pipe, expansion tank and vent or wiring.

  4. STEP 04

    Strap, fill and fire.

    Straps go on, the tank fills with air bled out, then the unit is lit or powered and tested for leaks and temperature.

  5. STEP 05

    Walkthrough.

    You learn where the shutoffs are, how to read the temperature setting and when to flush the tank.
