What plumbing installation covers

Plumbing installation in San Juan Capistrano, CA means the work of putting new pipes, fixtures and connections where none existed or where old ones are being replaced. It covers a new bathroom, a kitchen remodel, an added hose bib, a relocated washer, and the plumbing in a new building. It is planned work, so you set the scope and date. If something is leaking or backing up right now, call the 24/7 line at (949) 325-3122 instead.

Good installation work is mostly decided before any pipe is cut. The plumber asks where water enters, where it drains, and what the walls and floor allow. New lines and fixtures are set to the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and a licensed plumber knows where that code applies.

Remodel rough-in: the work behind the walls

Rough-in is the plumbing done while walls are open: supply lines, drain pipes, vents and shutoffs placed where the future fixtures will sit. Once drywall goes up, changes get expensive. A fixture that moves six inches after rough-in can mean reopening the wall.

Bring fixture specs to the planning visit, because rough-in dimensions depend on the exact sink, vanity, tub or toilet. For a bathroom swap, our toilet work page shows how a setting is handled, and the same thinking applies to everything else in the room.

Tub and shower installs

Tubs and showers add the most hidden work. The drain must slope properly, the valve must sit at the right depth for the finished wall, and a pan or liner has to be tested before tile goes on. A leak found after tiling means breaking the finished surface.

  • Choose the valve and trim before rough-in, since the depth depends on the model.
  • Decide whether the old drain location is staying.
  • Confirm the subfloor and framing can carry the new tub.
  • Fill-test the pan or tub before finishing the wall.

New construction and added water lines

In a new building, plumbing is laid out in stages: underground drain and supply first, then rough-in inside framed walls, then fixtures at the end. Each stage waits on an inspection. Which inspections your project needs is a question for the city's building department, since they change with the job.

A new water line, such as one to a detached garage, casita or outdoor kitchen, starts with where it tees off the existing supply. Distance, trench path and the available supply size all affect the work. Hard water averaging about 12 grains per gallon in the area is a good reason to install shutoff valves at every branch and to plan a heater with a drain valve for flushing scale.

Gas line safety comes first

If you smell gas

Leave the building at once. Do not flip switches, use a phone inside, or start a car in an attached garage. From a safe distance outside, call the gas utility or 911 BEFORE you call any plumber.

A gas smell is not a plumbing appointment, it is a safety event. The utility or fire department checks the air and shuts off supply when needed. Once the area is declared safe, a plumber can repair or extend the line. Gas line work needs a permit and inspection, and we never promise a response time on a gas call. The city's building department can tell you what your project requires.
