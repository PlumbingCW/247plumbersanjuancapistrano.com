What is in the water coming to your house

Water softener and filtration service in San Juan Capistrano, CA starts with knowing which water you have. Santa Margarita Water District serves the city with a blend: local groundwater treated at the San Juan Groundwater Plant, water purchased from Irvine Ranch Water District and water from the Metropolitan Water District, which is imported from the Colorado River and State Water Project. The mix changes what comes out of your tap.

In the 2026 report (2025 data), total hardness averaged 210 mg/L, which is about 12 grains per gallon, and readings spread from 89 to 290 mg/L. Even the low end is moderately hard, and the swing from 5.2 to 17 grains means two neighbors can need different equipment. Test your own tap or ask SMWD Customer Care about your part of the system.

Sizing a softener from your grains

A softener is sized by how many grains of hardness your household uses between regenerations. Multiply daily water use by your hardness in grains per gallon. A family of four using about 75 gallons each at 12 grains faces roughly 3,600 grains a day, so a unit rated near 32,000 grains would regenerate about every nine days.

Too small a unit regenerates constantly and wastes salt and water. Too large a unit can sit idle so long that the resin gets stale. If your address leans toward the 17-grain end of the district's range, the same family needs a larger unit than a neighbor near 5 grains. Softener drain lines and bypass plumbing also fall under the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Salt-based softener or salt-free conditioner

These two do different jobs. A salt-based softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium on a resin bed, so the water is genuinely soft. A salt-free conditioner changes how the minerals behave so they cling less to pipes, but the hardness number does not drop.

At the district's 12-grain average, most households who want a real change in how water feels and how appliances age lean toward salt-based. Where salt use or discharge limits worry you, ask about alternatives before buying. Water heaters appreciate soft water too, and our tankless service page shows how scale affects them.

Where filtration fits

Filtration is a separate job from softening. A whole-house carbon filter takes out chlorine taste and odor, a sediment filter catches grit and an under-sink reverse osmosis unit polishes drinking water. Softeners do none of these things, so pair them according to what bothers you.

Decide by symptom. If glass is cloudy and showers feel filmy, you want softening. If water tastes or smells off, you want carbon. If you only care about drinking and cooking, a single tap-side filter may be enough.

What hard water looks like in the house

  • White crust builds up on my faucets and showerheads.
  • Dishes come out of the dishwasher with spots.
  • Soap does not lather and my skin feels dry after showering.
  • The water heater rumbles or my last one died early.
  • Laundry feels stiff and towels look dull.
  • Water pressure at showerheads is dropping slowly.

Water conditioner repair

Softeners fail in ordinary ways. Resin beads break down, the brine tank forms a salt bridge so no brine reaches the resin, the control valve sticks or the injector clogs. Water that suddenly turns hard, a unit running endlessly or a flooded brine tank usually points to one of these.

We check the salt level and bridge first, then run a manual regeneration and test hardness before and after. If a unit is beyond repair, we explain what the replacement should look like, sized from the numbers above. Salt-free conditioners need their media replaced on a schedule, and we handle that too.
