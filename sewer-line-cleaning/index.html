Why every drain slows down together

Branch lines carry waste from each fixture into one pipe, the main. If the main is partly blocked, the branches empty slowly or not at all. A toilet that bubbles when the shower runs, or a tub that fills when the washer drains, is a classic sign of a main-line clog.

A single slow sink is different and belongs under drain cleaning. The key is the pattern: several fixtures, lowest ones worst. If you can say that to the dispatcher in one sentence, the right machine arrives.

The clean-out: your line's front door

A clean-out is a capped pipe, usually outside near the house or at the property line, that gives direct access to the sewer. With it, the plumber feeds a machine straight in without pulling a toilet. Without one, access means removing a fixture or cutting in a new clean-out, which affects the time and price.

Knowing where yours is helps. If the cap sits under a bush or is buried, tell the dispatcher. Never open it yourself when sewage is already backing up, because pressure can send waste out in a rush.

Root cutting inside the main

Roots grow toward the moisture that leaks from joints and cracks. Inside the main they spread like hair, catching paper and growing thicker each season. A sewer machine with a cutter head, often a heavy chain or blades on a large cable, saws through the mass and pulls the pieces out.

Cutting buys time, not a cure. The root outside the pipe is still alive. When a line fills with roots again and again, sewer line repair is the lasting answer, and jetting cleans the pipe wall once the roots are cut.

Signs the main needs cleaning

  • Toilets gurgle when another fixture drains.
  • The lowest shower or floor drain shows dirty water first.
  • A sewer smell outside near the clean-out.
  • Water backs up in the tub when the washing machine runs.
  • Several drains are slow, and none of them improved with a plunger.

If you see two of these together, treat it as a main-line problem and avoid using more water. A cleaning is usually quicker than a person expects when the clean-out is easy to reach.

Floor drains and other low sewer connections

A basement or garage floor drain connects directly to the sewer, so it is the first place a main-line backup shows up. Even without a blockage, a dry trap can let sewer gas in. Cleaning the connected line, sometimes called sewer drain cleaning, clears the same route and checks that the trap holds water.

Where there is no floor drain, the same backup shows up at a ground-floor tub or shower instead. Either way, the plumber starts from the clean-out and works toward the house.
