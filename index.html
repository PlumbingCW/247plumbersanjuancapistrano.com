The first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

Water does most of its damage in the first few minutes, so what you do before the plumber arrives matters. Find your main shutoff valve, often near the water meter, on the front wall of the house or in the garage, and turn it clockwise until it stops. Then call (949) 325-3122, our 24/7 line, and say what you see and hear.

  1. Turn the main water valve fully clockwise. If it will not budge, note where it is and tell us on the call.
  2. Stay out of rooms where water is touching outlets, switches or a ceiling fixture that is filling up.
  3. Shut off the water heater (gas control to off, or the breaker for an electric unit) if the tank is leaking.
  4. Stop flushing and running drains if sewage or gray water is coming up. Each flush adds to the mess.
  5. Lift rugs, electronics and paperwork off wet floors, and take photos for your insurer.
  6. Call (949) 325-3122 and describe the problem so the right plumber is sent.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 first. A plumbing call can wait until everyone is outside and safe.

Drain and sewer trouble

A slow bathtub and a gurgling toilet usually point to different places. One clog sits in a single fixture's trap or branch line, and the other sits in the main line that carries the whole house to the street. Drain cleaning with a cable machine clears most single-fixture stoppages, while hydro-jetting sends high-pressure water down the pipe to scour grease and scale off the walls instead of punching one hole through the blockage.

When several drains slow at the same time, suspect the main. Sewer line cleaning opens it, and a camera run during a sewer inspection shows whether roots, a sag in the pipe or a cracked joint caused the stoppage. If the camera finds a break, sewer line repair is the next conversation, and you hear the options and the price before work starts.

Leaks, slab trouble and burst pipes

Not every leak announces itself. A water bill that climbs with no change in habits, a hiss when every fixture is off, or a warm patch on a tile floor all suggest water escaping somewhere hidden. Leak detection uses listening gear and pressure checks to find the spot before anyone cuts into a wall. When the leak is under the concrete, slab leak repair can mean rerouting the line overhead instead of breaking up the floor.

A split pipe is a different kind of day. Water pours, a ceiling stains and sags, and the main valve is your best friend. Burst pipe repair starts with stopping the flow, then cutting out the failed section and replacing it. Hard freezes are rare on this coast, so splits here more often trace to age, corrosion or a bad fitting.

Water heater trouble, from lukewarm to leaking

A tank heater that runs out fast, rumbles, or leaves a wet ring on the floor is telling you something. Water heater repair covers failed elements, thermostats, gas valves and the T&P valve, the safety valve that releases pressure if the tank overheats. If the tank itself is leaking, repair is off the table and water heater replacement is the answer.

Tank heaters last roughly a decade, and hard water can shorten that by building scale on the bottom. A move to a new spot or a different fuel is a job for water heater installation, which includes gas, venting and the earthquake strapping California requires. If endless hot water appeals to you, ask about a tankless water heater, and plan on regular descaling in a hard water area.

Toilets, faucets, disposals and new fixtures

Smaller failures cost sleep too. A toilet that refills all night wastes water, and toilet repair or installation covers flappers, fill valves, wax rings and full replacements. A dripping spout is usually a worn cartridge or washer, which faucet repair fixes without replacing the fixture. A disposal that hums and will not spin needs garbage disposal repair, and please keep your hand out of it.

Planned work goes through plumbing installation: new sinks, dishwasher and ice maker lines, shutoff valves, hose bibs and remodel rough-ins. These jobs are scheduled rather than rushed, so the plumber can see the space first and give you a clear price before work starts.

Commercial buildings, repipes and water treatment

A closed restroom or a backed-up kitchen line costs a business money by the hour. Commercial plumbing covers restaurants, offices, retail and rental buildings, and property managers handling several units can use the same dispatch line at (949) 325-3122.

Some pipe problems belong to the whole system, not one spot. When pinhole leaks keep returning in different places, repiping replaces the worn runs so you stop paying for repairs on a failing network. And because local water is hard, water softener and filtration work cuts down scale on fixtures and appliances, which is worth discussing before you buy your next water heater.

Signs you should call today

Homeowners describe these problems in their own words, and the words are usually accurate. If any of these sound familiar, do not wait for a bigger bill.

  • Water is coming through my ceiling or down a wall.
  • Every drain is slow, and the toilet gurgles when the shower runs.
  • There is a sewage smell near a cleanout in the yard or garage.
  • The water heater is wet underneath, or it pops and rumbles.
  • The water meter keeps moving even though everything is off.
  • A spot on the floor feels warm and it was not warm last week.
  • There is no hot water at all and the pilot will not stay lit.

Hard water, old adobe and the roads we drive

Water service in town moved to Santa Margarita Water District (SMWD) in November 2021. The district's 2026 report puts hardness at an average near 12 grains per gallon, with readings from about 5 to 17 depending on where you sit in the system. That is hard water, so expect scale on heater elements and spotted fixtures, and ask SMWD Customer Care about your own address.

Around the Mission and Los Rios Street, the neighborhood includes restored board-and-batten buildings from the 19th century. Older buildings can hide older plumbing, and every structure is different, so the plumber diagnoses first and prices second. If you are remodeling a historic property, ask the San Juan Capistrano building department about permit rules before work starts.

Crews leave from our office on Del Obispo Street. I-5 crosses the city and Ortega Highway meets it at the SR-74 interchange, so crews reach most streets without long detours. Heavy highway traffic or a parade day can slow any crew, which is one reason we never promise arrival times.

The mild coastal climate, about 13.6 inches of rain a year, means most plumbing trouble here comes from wear and mineral scale rather than freezing. That also means a slow, steady leak can hide for weeks, so check your meter if the bill jumps.
